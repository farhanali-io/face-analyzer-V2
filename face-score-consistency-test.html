Two columns, kept apart

Most face-rating tools collapse the face and the photograph into one number. Keeping them in separate columns is the entire point of this test.

01

Face geometry per photo

The part of the score that describes your face — symmetry, proportion and feature relationships — measured independently for each photo you upload.

02

Photo presentation per photo

The part that describes the photograph — lighting, sharpness, head angle, framing and contrast — kept in a separate column so the two never blur together.

03

Range, not just values

Both columns report a spread. A narrow face-geometry range next to a wide presentation range is the clearest possible answer to why a score moved.

How the consistency test works

  1. 1. Upload two or three

    Use photos of the same face taken under whatever conditions you normally shoot in.

  2. 2. Measure identically

    Every photo goes through the same deterministic formulas — the same image always returns the same numbers.

  3. 3. Compare the spreads

    The face column and the photo column are compared, and the factors that varied most are ranked.

What the result cannot tell you

01

Face geometry is measured from a 2D photo, so it is not perfectly pose-independent. A large angle change moves it too, and the tool says so when it does.

02

Expression is a genuine change to the measured geometry, not a photographic artefact — a broad smile will legitimately shift the numbers.

03

Three photos is a small sample. It shows whether variation exists, not a statistically robust estimate of it.

04

A stable geometry score is not a statement that your face is symmetrical, attractive or unchanging — only that this measurement repeated.

How local processing works →
Why does my attractiveness score change between photos?+

Because most face-rating tools mix two different things into one number: the geometry of your face and the quality of the photograph. Lighting, focus, head angle and camera distance can move a combined score by 15 points or more while the face itself is unchanged. This test separates the two so you can see which one actually moved.

Is a face rating supposed to be the same every time?+

The calculation should be. Our formulas are deterministic: feeding the same processed image in twice returns identical numbers, with no randomness. What changes between your photos is the input, not the model. Tools that return wildly different results for the same image are doing something else.

What counts as a stable result?+

A face-geometry range of three points or less across your photos is treated as stable here. Up to seven is within what pose and expression alone typically produce. More than that usually means a large change in angle, expression or camera distance between the shots.

How many photos should I use?+

Two is enough to see whether variation exists. Three gives a better sense of the range, especially if you deliberately vary one thing at a time — same pose in different light, or same light at different angles.

Are my photos uploaded?+

No. Every image is decoded and measured inside this browser tab. Nothing is transmitted, and everything is discarded when you reset or leave the page.
