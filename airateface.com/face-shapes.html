Oval face shape

Moderately long, with the forehead a little wider than the jaw and a softly rounded chin.

Round face shape

Similar in length and width, with a soft jaw that curves instead of turning a corner.

Square face shape

Short and wide like a round face, but with a jaw that turns a clear corner below the ear.

Oblong face shape

The longest of the seven profiles, with widths that stay fairly even from forehead to jaw.

Heart face shape

A wide forehead narrowing to a pointed chin, often with a widow's peak.

Diamond face shape

Widest at the cheekbones, with both a narrow forehead and a narrow, pointed chin.

Compare similar face shapes

Start with the feature that best separates two close matches.

Round vs square

Start with the jaw corner. Both profiles can be short and wide; a round outline curves through the corner, while a square outline turns more distinctly.

Width measurements alone can leave these two close. Our shape matcher also uses jaw and chin angles.

See the difference →

Oval vs oblong

Look at length relative to cheek width. Both contours taper softly, but the oblong profile is longer. Our reference length/width values are 1.44 for oval and 1.64 for oblong.

Those values describe this tool's profiles; they are not hard boundaries or population norms.

See the difference →

Heart vs diamond

Compare forehead width with cheek width. Both can taper toward a pointed chin. A heart outline is wider at the forehead; a diamond is widest at the cheeks.

Keep the temples visible. Hair can hide the width that helps distinguish these profiles.

See the difference →

Measure first, then explore your style

The photo tool compares face length, forehead, cheek and jaw widths, plus jaw and chin angles. These are visible landmarks, not direct measurements of bone or an exact hairline. A manual measurement option is available in the detector.
