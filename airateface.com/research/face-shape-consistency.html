Round or square? Test the face-shape boundary

Can two contours with identical width ratios receive different face-shape labels?

By AI Rate FaceShape-classifier sensitivity experimentCalculated September 8, 2026

What this experiment shows

The leading match moves from Square toward Round as the jaw corner softens, even though all three width ratios are unchanged. Near the transition, the runner-up remains useful information rather than an error to hide.

Inputs and method

We fix face length / cheek width at 1.26, forehead / cheek width at 0.89, jaw / cheek width at 0.91, and chin angle at 116°. We vary only the jaw corner angle from 132° to 160° in 4° steps. Each row uses the production seven-profile matcher, including its weights and rounding.

Calculation version: ARF calculation experiments 1.0. Engine: ARF Local Geometry v3.0.

How to use this when reading your result

Use the detector as a guide to a contour, then read both leading profiles when they are close. For hairstyles or glasses, you can borrow ideas from both. Pulling hair away from the outline and using a consistent frontal photo makes comparisons easier, but does not turn a styling category into a biological fact.

What this experiment cannot tell you

These are chosen inputs to hand-set profiles, not a study of labelled faces. Match shares are normalized model similarities, not calibrated probabilities that a person belongs to a category. The boundary belongs to this version of this calculator.

Sources and calculation choices

This experiment tests AI Rate Face's own hand-set shape profiles. It has no external population dataset or clinical reference. The exact profiles and formula are included in the downloadable calculation code.
