Italian Bob by Face Shape

A full bob around the jaw, with a substantial perimeter and softly curved ends.

The useful decision is where the full edge of the bob should sit on you. A jaw-length perimeter and a below-chin perimeter put the widest part of the haircut in different places. Your face-shape result can help you compare those options; your texture and routine decide whether they are practical.

Italian Bob variations for seven face shapes

Use these suggestions to choose where the haircut adds width or leaves the outline visible. You can prefer a different effect.

Oval face

Start around the jaw and adjust the part to your growth pattern; keep the perimeter full without adding unnecessary layers.

Round face

Try a perimeter below the chin and less volume at the cheeks. Keep the front slightly longer if you want a more vertical outline.

Square face

Let the front end a little below the jaw and soften the ends so the cut does not repeat the same horizontal jaw line.

Oblong face

Keep the length near the jaw and allow side fullness. A longer collarbone version changes the silhouette and may add more apparent length.

Heart face

Keep fullness near the chin to give the lower outline more width, while leaving the crown relatively flat.

Diamond face

Keep the perimeter full below the cheekbones and use a soft part so the widest section is not at the cheeks.

Triangle face

Avoid concentrating a blunt, heavy edge exactly on the jaw. Try a slightly longer front and lighter ends.

Choose the perimeter before the part

Start by showing your stylist where you want the ends to land when the hair is dry. A full edge near the chin adds a visible horizontal line around the lower face. Taking that edge below the chin moves the line downward. Neither length is universally better; choose the effect you want.

Try your usual part while discussing the cut. A different part may look good in a reference photo but require more styling if it works against a cowlick. Keep the hair behind and in front of your ears during the consultation so you can see how both versions sit.

Match the weight to your hair

Fine hair may lose the full perimeter if too much weight is removed. Dense hair may need internal shaping to keep the lower section from becoming a solid block. These are different cutting decisions even when both clients ask for an Italian bob.

With waves or curls, choose the outline using the dry shape as well as the stretched length. Ask what the cut will look like when you air-dry it, rather than assuming the rounded finish in a photograph happens without styling.

Decide how much styling you want to do

A full bob can work with natural movement, but a polished curved-under finish may need a brush and dryer. If you prefer air-drying, bring a photo of your normal texture to the appointment and ask for a version that works in that state.

Before cutting, check whether you need to tie your hair back and how often you want to trim the outline. A collarbone lob may serve that routine better, although it creates a different silhouette from a jaw-length Italian bob.

Questions about italian bob

Can a round face wear an Italian bob?

Yes. If you want less visible cheek width, compare a slightly longer front and a perimeter below the chin. A shorter, fuller version is also a valid choice if you like that shape.

Is an Italian bob always chin-length?

The name is used for several full bob variations. Agree on the actual dry length and weight with your stylist instead of relying on the name alone.

Will this tool show the haircut on my face?

No. It compares your face-shape result with our styling rules and suggests a variation. The reference portrait is an AI-generated illustration, not a preview of your haircut.

Before your appointment

  • Bring a photo of your usual, unstyled hair.
  • Choose the length you need for tying or tucking back.
  • Ask to see how the ends sit when dry.
  • Discuss styling time and regular trims.

Maintenance: medium. A full perimeter needs regular trims. Air-drying works best when your natural texture already falls into the intended shape.
