Evidence behind this result

The output is built from inspectable photo geometry and a separate image-quality signal.

01

Paired landmarks

Compares horizontal distance from the facial midline and vertical alignment for matched left-right points.

02

Five regions

Separates the eyes, brows, nose, mouth and jaw so one area cannot hide another.

03

Pose signal

Uses the eye line and nose offset to flag roll or yaw that can distort the estimate.

How this tool works

  1. 1. Detect

    A browser-based model finds one face and its visible landmark coordinates.

  2. 2. Align

    The eye line defines a rotated coordinate system for left-right comparisons.

  3. 3. Compare

    Normalised regional differences become an explainable 0–100 geometry score.

What the result cannot tell you

01

Expression, lens distortion, lighting and head rotation can change apparent symmetry.

02

Human faces are naturally asymmetric; a higher score is not a health or beauty diagnosis.

03

The tool measures a single two-dimensional photo, not bone structure or three-dimensional anatomy.

About this tool

How local processing works →
Is a perfectly symmetrical face the goal?+

No. Natural asymmetry is normal and can be visually distinctive. This tool only describes visible paired geometry in one photo.

Why does my score change between photos?+

Camera distance, head angle, expression and lighting move visible landmarks. Use the same neutral, front-facing setup for comparison.

Does the test upload my photo?+

No. You choose a photo, but it is never sent anywhere: the tool loads its model into your browser and performs the measurement on your device.
