How the detector tells close face shapes apart

An oval and oblong face can taper similarly; face length helps separate them. Round and square contours can have similar widths; the jaw corner becomes more useful. The result ranks all seven profiles so you can compare the leading matches.

How this tool works

  1. 1. Trace

    Visible face-oval and key width landmarks define the photo contour.

  2. 2. Measure

    Three scale-independent width ratios and two contour angles are taken from the landmark set.

  3. 3. Rank

    Each of the seven profiles is scored on all five measurements and converted into a match percentage.

About this tool

How local processing works →
Can I have more than one face shape?+

Yes. Face shape is continuous, which is why this detector ranks all seven profiles with a percentage each. When the top two are within about ten points, both are reasonable descriptions of the same face.

How is a square face different from a round one?+

Not by width. Both are relatively short and wide, so the difference is the jaw corner: a square contour turns sharply toward the chin, a round one curves. This detector measures that angle directly rather than inferring it from width ratios.

Should I pull my hair back?+

If possible. A clear forehead, cheek and jaw outline gives the detector better visible evidence, and the forehead width is the measurement hair affects most.

Is the result stored?+

Detection runs locally. A summary of the seven shape weights and five measurements stays in this tab for up to 30 minutes so hairstyle and glasses pages can reuse it. The summary does not include your photo or landmarks. You can clear it from the result.
